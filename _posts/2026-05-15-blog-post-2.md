## History
Most of the people who take some courses in probabilty and statistics come to know about much celebrated **normal distibution** or **Gaussian distribution**. As it is a global attractor for all the distributions under sum of random variables as limit goes to $$\lim_{n \to \infty} X_1 + X_2 + ... + X_n$$, and underlying distribtion doesn't matter, given that varaince and mean of underlying distribution is finite. This can be proved using the generating function method when the distribution is **iid** (independent and identically distributed). Core philosophy behind Central Limit Theorem is that effects of the random functions gets normalised on an average, and eventually we get this world full of Gaussian distributions ([More on this](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HBluLfX2F_k)).
One specific example of this is ideal gas in a box, if start with some distribution of speeds for gas molecules, doesn't matter what initial distribution we start with, eventually distribution of speeds approaches to [**Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell–Boltzmann_distribution) which is Gaussian in 3D speeds space. And vague reason again remains the same, as time evolves particles collide with each other and effects gets normalised on an average.

Now the speed distribution of gas historically was first derived by James Clerk Maxwell in 1860 with purely mathematical reasoning, later Ludwig Boltzmann derived it with much more physical reasoning(though mathematically involved) in 1872 with famous his famous **Boltmann-equation**, and his approach was from a point of view evolution of state in time. Eventually it bears the name of both of the great minds of 19th century.

Here i want to discuss the Maxwell's approach, which is simple in considerations but heavy in the consequences in my opinion.

## Maxwell's reasoning
 In this [paper](http://www.alternativaverde.it/stel/documenti/Maxwell/1860/Maxwell%20%281860%29%20-%20Illustrations%20of%20the%20dynamical%20theory%20of%20gases.pdf) Maxwell was thinking about the velocity components of gas, and his first insight was that the distribution of the velocity components should be independent of each other, just think about two particles colliding facing each other, after collision they can not gain velocity in some different direction, thus we can conclude that x component of velocity doesn't affect the y or z component of velocity and vice-versa. 

now instead of writing $$v_x, v_y, v_z$$ each time i will just use $$x, y, z$$ bearing in mind that this represents velocity components. And with this we can write 

$$ p(x,y,z) = f(x) f(y) f(z) $$
where $$f(x)$$ is probability distribution of velocity component $$x$$, and because we assume it to have isotropy due to the physically, it shouldn't matter whether it is x direction or y or z.

2nd point of maxwell was, that there is nothing specific about choosing $$x$$, $$y$$ and $$z$$ in a particular directions in space, i can rotate them with some angles, and this should not affect my underlying distribution, means the distribution should be rotationally invaraint. With this one more contraint of roational invariance we are now forced to have function of just the metric in space, means only as the distance from the origin which is $$ \sqrt{x^2 +y^2 +z^2}$$, with this we arrive at the following relation,

$$f(x)f(y)f(z) = g(\sqrt{x^2 +y^2 +z^2}).$$


![Rotation Invariance Original Paper]({{ site.url }}/images/blogs/maxwell-herchel-distribution/rot_inv.png)
>Snippet from the original paper, i have assume it to be be $$\sqrt r := \sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}$$ instead of $$r^2$$, but it will make not much difference



Now to generalise the things there is noting special about dimension three, we can do apply the same logic for any arbitary dimentions $$d>2$$. Above relation follows for d dimentions


$$\Pi_{i=1}^{d}f(x_i) = g(\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^d x_i^2})$$



now first taking natural log on both sides and then taking the partial derivative wrt $$$x_i$$ on both sides, we get

$$\frac{f'(x_i)}{x_if(x_i)} = \frac{1}{g(\sqrt r)\sqrt r} = \frac{f'(x_k)}{x_kf(x_k)} = C$$

now one can argue taking the derivate wrt $$x_i$$ imposes the condition of $$f(x_i)$$ being smooth and continuous. From above equation now 

$$ \int \frac{df}{f(x_i)} = \int Cx_idx_i$$

$$ f(x_i) = A e^{Cx_i^2} $$

Now keeping in mind that $$ p(x, y, z) = f(x)f(y)f(z)$$ for the 3d case, we get

$$ p(x, y, z) = A^3e^{C(x^2+y^2+z^2)}$$, because it is a probability distribution, it should be bounded within the distribution limits $$-\infty$$ to $$+\infty$$, which will force us to take the $$C  \to -C$$, to keep the distribution bounded and normalizable. If we are to Normalse it we will find that $$ A = \sqrt{C/\pi}$$, and if we take C to be according to the definition of our gaussian distribution's standard deviation which is $$ C = \sqrt{1/2\sigma^2}$$, we get 

$$ p(x, y, z) = \frac{1}{({\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2})^3}}e^{-\frac{x^2+y^2+z^2}{2\sigma^2}} $$

Thus we get out much celebrated maxwell boltzmann distribution, we were to represent it in polar coordinates.

$$ p(r) =  4\pi \frac{r^2}{({\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2})^3}}e^{-\frac{r^2}{2\sigma^2}}$$

and $$4\pi$$ is coming from the fact the we intregrate $$\theta$$ and $$\phi$$ . This matches the formula of Boltmann distribution, later boltzmann with his theory of kinetic gases fixed constant $$\sigma$$ and found the connection between temperature, which is story for another day. 

But here my main objective was just to show that the **Gassian distribution** can be derived, with independence and rotational invariance.

## History
Most of the people who take some courses in probabilty and statistics come to know about much celebrated **normal distibution** or **Gaussian distribution**. As it is a global attractor for all the distributions under sum of random variables as limit goes to $$\lim_{n \to \infty} X_1 + X_2 + ... + X_n$$, and underlying distribtion doesn't matter, give that varaince and mean of underlying distribution is finite. This can be proved using the generating function method when the distribution is **iid** (independent and identically distributed). Core philosophy behind Central Limit Theorem is that effects of the random functions gets normalised on an average, and eventually we get this world full of Gaussian distributions ([More on this](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HBluLfX2F_k)).
One specific example of this is ideal gas in a box, if start with some distribution of speeds for gas molecules, doesn't matter what initial distribution we start with, eventually distribution of speeds approaches to [**Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell–Boltzmann_distribution) which is Gaussian in 3D speeds space. And vague reason again remains the same, as time evolves particles collide with each other and effects gets normalised on an average.

Now the speed distribution of gas historically was first derived by James Clerk Maxwell in 1860 with purely mathematical reasoning, later Ludwig Boltzmann derived it with much more physical reasoning in 1872 with famous his famous **Boltmann-equation**, and his approach was from a point of view evolution of state in time. Eventually it bears the name of both of the great minds of 19th century.

Here i want to discuss the Maxwell approach, which is simple in considerations but heavy in the consequences in my opinion.

## Maxwell's reasoning
 In this [paper](http://www.alternativaverde.it/stel/documenti/Maxwell/1860/Maxwell%20%281860%29%20-%20Illustrations%20of%20the%20dynamical%20theory%20of%20gases.pdf) Maxwell was thinking about the velocity components of gas, and his first insight was that the distribution of the velocity components should be independent of each other, just think about two particles colliding facing each other, after collision they can not gain velocity in some different direction, thus we can conclude that x component of velocity doesn't affect the y or z component of velocity and vice-versa. 

now instead of writing $$v_x, v_y, v_z$$ each time i will just use $$x, y, z$$ bearing in mind that this represents velocity components. And with this we can write 

$$ P(x,y,z) = f(x) f(y) f(z) $$
where $f(x)$ is probability distribution of velocity component $x$.
